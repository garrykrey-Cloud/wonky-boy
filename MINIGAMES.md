# Wonky Boy — 100 mini-game encounters

Rooms placed in the maze, 4 to 6 per board. Entering one expands it to fill the
screen; the exits stay visible and usable throughout, and leaving shrinks the
board back to normal.

Status: **awaiting approval**. Nothing here is implemented yet.

Tick the ones you want. Strike out the ones you do not.

---

## Ground rules these were written against

Every encounter has to work with one thumbstick and a boy who does not obey it.
That rules out anything needing precise aim, fast taps, or more than one input.
It also means difficulty comes for free — the sloppiness is the challenge, so
most rooms only need a simple goal and an obstacle.

Each is meant to resolve in **10 to 30 seconds**. Anything longer stops being a
punctuation mark and becomes a level.

## Decisions needed before any of this is built

These change the design of every room, so they are worth settling first.

1. **What does winning give you?** A shield, a shortcut, a cosmetic unlock, or
   just the satisfaction? This ties directly to the in-app purchase question -
   if rooms hand out advantages, selling those advantages becomes loaded.
2. **What does losing cost?** Nothing, a zap back to the board start, or does
   the room simply close? "Nothing" makes rooms free to attempt and removes any
   reason to avoid them.
3. **Can a room be skipped?** The brief says the exits stay usable throughout,
   which implies yes - walk in, walk straight out. Worth confirming that is
   intended, because it makes every room optional.
4. **Are they once-only?** Does a cleared room stay cleared when you die and
   restart the board, or does it reset with everything else?

## Scope, honestly

A hundred approved concepts is a fine thing to have. A hundred *implemented*
mini-games is not a feature, it is a second game - each needs its own rules,
art, failure states, tuning and testing.

Suggested approach: approve the full list as a backlog, then pick **8 to 12**
to build first, spread across categories so the variety is obvious from the
start. Board generation can draw from whatever exists and grow later without
any structural change.

Good first tranche, if you want a recommendation: 1, 11, 21, 31, 41, 49, 51,
61, 71, 91 — one from each category, all mechanically distinct, none needing
new systems beyond the room itself.
